# Outline

Main idea: Designers can ritualize time, focusing on depth rather than productive clock-time (Han). Relying on foreground/background-shifting (Eno) as well as repetition, returning, and ceremonious intervals (Vesper5/Ritual of the Moon).  

## Intro - Tati's Playtime

- Description of the exterior apartment scene from Jacques Tati's "Playtime."
- Background on why Tati made Playtime
- "Tati filmed his movie in 70mm, that grand epic format that covers the largest screens available with the most detail imaginable. He shot entirely in medium-long and long shots; no closeups, no reaction shots, no over the shoulder. He shows us the big picture all of the time, and our eyes dart around it to find action in the foreground, middle distance, background and half-offscreen. It is difficult sometimes to even know what the subject of a shot is;" - Ebert
- "Playtime" as a new way to engage with time and space in film

## Theory - Han's _The Scent of Time_

- Han's description of the fragrant clock
- How the fragrant clock differs from the standard perception of time
- Stewart Brand on Kairos vs Chronos
- Loss of time in Casinos?
- Similarly, games (and most media?) try to produce a flow state that erodes our perception of time
- Braxton's "Against Flow" argument
- What if we created games that brought a Kairos to the foreground

## Examples

We can see this happening in other creative media

- Recorded Music - Eno's "Music for Airports" (and ambient music in general) seeks to bring us in and out of kairos by creating music that can shift from foreground to background perception.
- Live Music - Cage's "As Slow as Possible" and "4:33" do two opposite things. "As slow as possible" forces us to engage with the breadth of time, while "4:33" focuses us on the immediate sounds around us.
- Theatre - Einstein on the Beach uses repetitive motifs and actions
- Film - Tati, but also films like Warhol's "Empire", Marclay's "Clock," and the slow cinema movement exemplified by Andrei Tarkovsky

It also has been experimented with in some games

- "The Longing" crystalizes the human-scale of time in a game mechanic. 
- "Vesper5" makes a game of a small (hopefully daily) intentional action.
- Ritual of the Moon does... something
- Can we better ritualize time in a different way to bring the player into a contemplative mode by yes/and-ing the above games? 

## Experiments

To be fleshed out further (paragraph by paragraph), but for now:

- Instauratio Exiguus introduces the idea of repetitive acts. "but a specific _kind_ of repetition. A repetition informed by ritual and attentiveness. Obviously a certain attention is required in this game, as it is in most (all?) games, but the obfuscation of the interface, where the player has to rely more heavily on sound and subtle changes in the visuals, cause that attention to be intensified." - MRB
- LAUTIR forces a daily, timed, interaction that itself brings the players relationship to time to the foreground. They are forced to wait, and that waiting can be difficult.
